Then, for the latter part of my gap year, I worked on a completely different question. Throughout my work in financial literacy, I had assumed ignorance was the disease and information the cure. I am still running Edunomix and Decoded, but I had started to see that literacy is only one part of the story.

So once I landed back in Delhi from Ashoka, I began exploring that full time: my research examines the relationship between fear of missing out (FOMO) and the sharing of financial misinformation on Indian social media. Specifically, I am investigating whether individuals who experience higher levels of FOMO are more likely to share misleading financial content, particularly when it creates a sense of urgency or suggests that they might miss an investment opportunity.

The study builds on a framework developed by Professor Hemant Kakkar, Associate Professor of Management at the Indian School of Business (ISB), on the psychological drivers of fake-news sharing. Under his guidance, I adapted the framework to the context of financial misinformation and incorporated the 10-item FOMO scale developed by Przybylski et al. (2013).

Check out the GitHub here! I am still in the middle of this study, and I am moving on to the analysis component of the study :)
