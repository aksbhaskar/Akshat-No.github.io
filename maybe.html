Hi, I'm Akshat.

I like building things and dragging good ideas out into the open where people can use them.

I'm interested in economics, anthropology, philosophy, and mathematics, and mostly in the questions that sit where they overlap. The one that has pulled at me longest is simple to state and hard to answer: how can young people, and above all those in under-resourced contexts, gain access to the knowledge and agency that shape their futures? Most of what I've built has been an attempt to answer it.

It started with money, and with how rarely anyone is taught how it works. While still in school, I founded the India chapter of the Edunomix Institute, a US 501(c)(3) nonprofit devoted to financial literacy, and built its first presence in South Asia. We ran a nationwide essay competition across nineteen states and fifty-four schools and held camps in personal finance. Through the Ukraine Speaks English initiative, in partnership with Learn and Teach UA, we also taught economics to displaced Ukrainian students whose ordinary schooling had been interrupted by the war. More than forty of them showed up for the first class of the latest cycle, logging on every week from halfway across the world just to learn how money actually works. That, more than any metric, is why the project exists.

The same conviction carried into TaxCity, a government-recognised card game that teaches tax filing, where I served as Chief Financial Officer. It reached more than 79,000 students across 27 countries, raised over $120,000 in grants, and earned recognition from the Ministry of Education. In the end, though, we had to wind it down. Even with the grants, giving away thousands of physical kits for free across India was no longer financially sustainable. We donated what remained to a few government schools we had visited, so the game could keep being played where it was needed most. I'm still proud of what it did.

I'm also drawn to public policy and social change from the inside. My research on the socio-economic dimensions of artificial intelligence, under Professor Surbhi Goel at the University of Pennsylvania, became a peer-reviewed paper published in IJRASET, which I presented at the NYC STEM Research Conference in 2025 as the only high-school student presenting.

During my gap year, I've been learning to fold software into that work. Under Professor Hemant Kakkar at the Indian School of Business, I'm studying how financial misinformation spreads across Indian social media. Because so much of the false content online is code-mixed Hindi and English in the same sentence, what we call "Hinglish," my analysis runs on language models built for Indian languages, MuRIL and XLM-RoBERTa, rather than the English-only tools most people reach for. Paired with validated psychological instruments, the BFI-2-XS personality inventory and the Fear of Missing Out scale, I'm chasing a sharper question than "which posts are false": which people are most likely to pass them on.

In June 2025, I served as a Policy Intern at the Ministry of Education, Government of India, in the office of Mr. Rahul Pachori, Director, working on internal analysis of the Samagra Shiksha Scheme, the country's flagship schooling programme under the National Education Policy 2020. Reading a national education budget line by line teaches you things a classroom cannot.

Underneath all of it runs something quieter. I love mathematics, especially pure mathematics and number theory. I was admitted to the Lodha Genius Programme at Ashoka University, a fully funded residential programme with an acceptance rate near three percent, where I did university-level coursework in number theory, group theory, linear algebra, and geometry, and sat in on the Great Ideas Seminars alongside Nobel laureates like Sir Paul Nurse and Professor Brian Schmidt. It was one of the most rewarding summers of my life, and I made a lot of brilliant friends there. In the first half of my gap year, I continued into original research through a residential mathematics apprenticeship under Professor Shanta Laishram at the Indian Statistical Institute, working in advanced number theory.

Ideas are only as useful as their reach. I write, and I host the Decoded Podcast, where we interview economists and technologists and translate their ideas and breakthroughs for a general audience. It has won three Spotify for Creators awards and passed three hundred listening hours, and I've built a small team around it. Recently I recorded a conversation I'd chased for weeks with the number theorist M. Ram Murty; it might be the best thing we've made.

I've also tried to spend time near people who build at scale. At Y Combinator's first Startup School India, I was one of two thousand chosen nationwide, pitched my financial-literacy work to YC partner Jared Friedman, and received an AI compute grant of over $25,000. Earlier, I was selected from a pool of thirty-two thousand into buildspace's s3 cohort.

My proudest moment came before I'd finished school, when I was felicitated by the President of India, Shri Droupadi Murmu, for my work on AI, one of seventeen students chosen nationwide. The rest of the record, ranks, olympiads, and programmes, is here.

Beyond the desk, I've volunteered with Familial Forestry, a reforestation movement recognised by the UN Land for Life Award, for five years, and I'm working to open a chapter at my school. I captained my school to back-to-back district football championships and served as President of the Student Council at Delhi Public School, R.K. Puram.
